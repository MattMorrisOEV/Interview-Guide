The short version

Octopus Electric Vehicles launched in 2018 with a simple mission: make switching to electric easy. We're not just a leasing company — we bundle the car, charger, and energy into one seamless deal, backed by technology we build ourselves.

Part of something bigger

Octopus Energy Group

We're part of Octopus Energy Group — one of the fastest-growing energy and technology companies in the world. The Group has been valued at over £4 billion and operates across multiple countries. That means we benefit from significant backing, shared engineering infrastructure, and a group-wide culture of doing things differently.

Why we exist

"From 2035, you can no longer buy a new petrol or diesel car in the UK. We're building the infrastructure, technology and experience to make that transition seamless for drivers."

The EV transition isn't just happening — it's accelerating. We sit at the centre of it, connecting drivers, energy, and technology in a way no one else does.

What we do
Core product

Salary sacrifice EV leasing

Our flagship product lets employees lease an electric vehicle through their employer via salary sacrifice — saving them tax and National Insurance. It's one of the most tax-efficient ways to drive electric in the UK, and we've built it into a seamless, end-to-end experience.

Technology

We build our own tech

Unlike most leasing companies, we have a substantial in-house engineering team. Our stack is Python with GraphQL on the backend, React with Next.js on the frontend, PostgreSQL and NoSQL databases, all running on AWS. If you're in a tech role, you'll be building real product — not maintaining legacy systems.

Scale & growth

We put roughly 3,000 vehicles on the road every month and manage a fleet of over 40,000 vehicles. That's real complexity — financing arrangements, securitisation facilities, high-volume financial data, and customer operations — all running through systems we own and improve.

Culture & values
Honest heads-up

We're a scale-up, not a corporate. Things move fast and aren't always perfectly structured. If you thrive in ambiguity and want to shape things, you'll love it. If you need everything defined for you, that's worth reflecting on before applying.

How we work

Autonomy over process

We trust people to get on with it. There isn't a rigid playbook for every situation — you're expected to use good judgement, flag problems early, and take ownership of outcomes. Managers set direction and get out of the way.

Customer obsession

The customer is always in the room

Every team — Tech, Finance, Operations, Commercial — is expected to connect their work to the customer experience. We're not a company that makes decisions in isolation from the people we serve.

Transparency

We say what we think

Octopus culture is unusually direct. Feedback flows both ways. You'll be expected to give honest views — including to people more senior than you — and to receive feedback with openness. Hierarchy exists, but it doesn't dominate.

Mission

The work matters

People here genuinely care about the mission. The EV transition is a real thing, and we're helping make it happen at scale. That's not a line in a slide deck — it shows up in how people talk about their work day to day.

What "ownership" looks like here

It's not just completing your tasks. It's flagging when something looks wrong even if it's not your area, thinking about what happens after your work lands, and not waiting to be told what to do when there's a clear problem. People who say "that's not my job" don't last long.

Pace

We move fast. Decisions get made quickly and things change. The people who thrive are energised by that, not stressed by it. If you've come from a large corporate with long approval chains, expect a significant change of pace — in a good way.

Who tends not to thrive

Those who need a lot of structure and instruction before acting. Those who wait to be told. Those who prioritise politics over output. Those who aren't genuinely interested in the mission. We're not for everyone — and that's fine.

A note on interviews

We're looking for real examples, not polished answers. Be specific about what you did — not "we". Be honest about what went wrong and what you learned. Candidates who try to sound impressive tend to come across worse than those who are just genuine.

Interview process
What to expect — typical stages

Stages vary by role. Your recruiter will confirm the exact process for your position.

1

Recruiter screen

30 mins with someone from the Talent team. Covering your background, motivations, salary expectations, and a chance for you to ask questions about the role and company.

2

Hiring manager interview

A deeper conversation about your experience and approach. Expect competency and values-based questions. Be ready with specific examples using the STAR method.

3

Task or technical stage

For many roles there'll be a practical element — a case study, technical task, or presentation. Your recruiter will give you full details in advance.

4

Final conversation

Often with a senior stakeholder. More about fit, ambition, and questions than grilling. A great chance to understand the bigger picture and make sure OEV is right for you too.

Tips for performing well
  • Use the STAR method: Situation, Task, Action, Result
  • Be specific — "we" is fine for context, but tell us what you did
  • It's okay to say "I don't know" — we value intellectual honesty
  • Come with questions — it shows genuine interest and helps you decide if we're right for you
  • Feedback is available at every stage — just ask your recruiter
Questions to ask us
About the role
  • What does success look like in the first 90 days?
  • What's the biggest challenge the team is working through right now?
  • How did this role come about — is it new or a backfill?
  • Who would I work most closely with day-to-day?
About culture & growth
  • How do people typically progress here?
  • What's been the biggest change in the business in the last 12 months?
  • How much autonomy will I have to shape how I work?
  • What do you wish you'd known before joining?
About the mission
  • Where do you see OEV in 3 years — product and team?
  • How does the team stay connected to what customers actually experience?
  • How does being part of Octopus Energy Group affect day-to-day work?
Questions to avoid

Avoid questions you could have googled. If you ask "what does Octopus EV do?" at final stage, it signals you haven't prepared. If in doubt, ask something about the team's experience or the future — those always land well.
